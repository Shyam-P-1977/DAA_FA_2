Algorithm Overview & College Viva Reference

Key concepts, mathematical guarantees, and formulas for DAA viva presentation.

1. Branch & Bound with Joint Charging

A min-priority queue explores delivery sequences ordered by lower bound. Rather than scheduling charging in a separate heuristic pass, charging stops are evaluated inside the Branch & Bound search state: whenever direct delivery violates the battery threshold rule, all reachable charging stations are expanded as candidate branches. All branches compete on the exact same admissible lower bound.

State = (cur_node, delivered_mask, battery_kWh) LB = cost_so_far + min(cur → remaining) + MST(remaining) + min(remaining → Hub) Admissibility: LB ≤ true_cost ⟹ Global optimality guaranteed!

2. Dijkstra's Algorithm on Road Network

The simulated city road network is represented as an undirected graph G=(V,E) with 8 vertices (Depot, 3 Deliveries, 3 Chargers, Hub) and non-negative weighted road edges. Dijkstra's algorithm finds the shortest path and sequence of road vertices between every pair of stops. The animated vehicle drives along the actual road segments one by one.

Dijkstra Shortest Path: O((V + E) log V) with min-heap Road Edges: 15 street segments matching the map layout Actual road sequences animated segment-by-segment.

3. Battery Decision Rule & 6 Outcomes

After each delivery (and initially at Depot), evaluate battery before selecting the next destination:

projected = battery_kWh − (distance / efficiency) 1. Battery above threshold: projected > T and ≥ R → proceed direct 2. Battery at threshold: projected == T → evaluate chargers 3. Battery below threshold: R ≤ projected < T → evaluate chargers 4. Insufficient battery: projected < R → evaluate chargers 5. No feasible charger: no station reachable → report infeasible 6. All deliveries completed → proceed to Hub

4. Why Nearest Charger is Not Always Best

A greedy approach of choosing the geographically closest charger can increase the total route cost or lead to an infeasible continuation. For example, a charger 2 km away might require a 15 km detour to reach the next delivery, whereas a charger 3 km away lies directly on the path to the delivery and requires only a 1 km detour. The optimizer evaluates the total continuation distance.

Min: detour(cur → charger) + distance(charger → next) Evaluated across all reachable stations in the B&B search.

5. Time & Space Complexity Summary

Branch & Bound Worst-Case: O(n! × |Chargers|ⁿ) MST Lower Bound (Prim): O(n²) per expanded node Dijkstra: O((V + E) log V) For n=3 orders: 3! = 6 permutations; pruned in <0.05 seconds.
